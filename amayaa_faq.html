🧵 About Our Sarees

Are your sarees genuine handloom?

Yes, every saree we sell is sourced directly from master weavers and established weaving clusters across India — Varanasi, Kanchipuram, Bishnupur, Chanderi, Pochampally, and more. We visit the weavers personally to ensure authenticity and fair trade.

What is the difference between Banarasi and Kanjivaram silk?

Banarasi silk (from Varanasi) is known for its rich brocade work with Mughal-inspired motifs — intricate jaal patterns and heavy zari. It's softer and drapes beautifully. Kanjivaram (from Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu) is heavier, known for bold temple borders, contrasting pallu, and vibrant colours with pure mulberry silk. Banarasi is ideal for North Indian weddings; Kanjivaram is the quintessential South Indian bridal saree.

Do the sarees come with a blouse piece?

Most of our sarees come with a matching unstitched blouse piece (typically 0.8–1 metre). This is mentioned in each product's description. If you need a separate blouse fabric or stitching assistance, please ask us — we can help you source a matching fabric.

What does GI-tagged mean?

A Geographical Indication (GI) tag is a legal certification that a product originates from a specific region and has qualities attributable to that origin. For example, Pochampally Ikat and Kanjivaram silk both carry GI tags — meaning only sarees genuinely woven in those regions can use that name. GI tags protect both the craft and the consumer.

Why do handloom sarees cost more than power-loom sarees?

A single Banarasi or Kanjivaram saree can take a master weaver anywhere from 2 weeks to 3 months to complete, depending on the complexity. The cost reflects the artisan's skill, the quality of raw silk and zari, and the heritage of the craft. When you buy handloom, you are directly supporting a weaver's livelihood and preserving a centuries-old art form.

✨ Saree Care

How should I wash a silk saree?

Silk sarees should ideally be dry cleaned for the first few washes to preserve the sheen and zari work. If hand-washing at home, use cold water with a mild silk-specific detergent — never wring or twist the fabric. Do not machine wash. Dry flat in shade, never in direct sunlight which can fade the colour.

How do I store a silk saree long-term?
  • Wrap in muslin cloth (not plastic) to allow the fabric to breathe
  • Store in a cool, dry, dark place away from moisture
  • Refold along different lines every 6 months to prevent permanent crease marks
  • Place neem leaves or silica gel packets nearby to deter insects
  • Never store with strong-smelling mothballs directly touching the saree
The zari (gold/silver thread) has tarnished — what do I do?

Tarnishing of zari is natural over time, especially with humidity. Avoid storing in humid conditions. To restore some shine, gently brush the zari area with a very soft brush. Professional dry cleaners with experience in silk sarees can also restore tarnished zari — ask specifically for zari treatment when giving it for dry cleaning.

How do I iron a silk saree?

Iron on low heat (silk setting) on the reverse side of the saree. Always place a thin cotton cloth between the iron and the saree. Never iron directly on zari work — the heat can damage the metallic threads. Steam ironing from a distance is a safer alternative.
